# Project 2 Regulatory Compliance — Current Internal Policies

# Internal AI-use policy

Policy version: `AI-POL-2026-08-15`

## Purpose

Quillhaven Academy uses AI only when the responsible owner can explain the use, preserve evidence, and route consequential decisions to an authorized human.

## Learner and public transparency

- A person directly interacting with an AI system must receive a clear notice before or at the first interaction unless the AI nature is obvious from the context.
- Public synthetic images, audio, or video must retain machine-readable provenance when the tool supports it. The Communications owner also requires a visible label unless Legal approves a documented exception.
- Staff-only drafting tools are not automatically treated as public interactions. The owner must still record how generated material reaches learners or the public.

## Evidence and exceptions

- Each registered system must have a current owner, use description, exposed group, disclosure status, human-review path, and evidence reference.
- `missing`, `stale`, `conflicting`, and `not-applicable` are different evidence states. Missing evidence must not be converted to compliance or non-compliance.
- An exception requires a named owner, rationale, expiry date, and Legal approval. The automation may draft an exception request but may not approve it.

## Approval boundary

Legal owns final interpretation of the EU AI Act. Operations owns policy activation and operational deadlines. System owners correct evidence. The automation may prepare a draft impact package and proposed actions; it may not give final legal advice, activate policy, change a production deadline, close an incident, or submit an official response.

## Operating scope

This review concerns Quillhaven Academy's EU programme team operating from Dublin, Ireland. That team administers all eight registered uses for its professional work; the learners, applicants and staff concerned include people in Ireland, and the public campaign and programme outputs are used there. None of these uses is personal, military, national-security or law-enforcement activity. The systems are operating services, not research-only prototypes.

For AI-001, AI-002, AI-003, AI-004, AI-006, AI-007 and AI-008, Quillhaven licenses and operates a third-party supplier's existing AI product. It did not develop or commission development of that product, distribute it as its own AI product, or place it into service under its own product name or trademark. ## Actual use and content

### AI-001

Learners exchange questions and generated replies directly with the licensed support chat. It does not classify biometric data or infer emotions or intentions. The existing notice and evidence reports remain the source for disclosure practice.

### AI-002

Staff use the drafting product privately. An admissions advisor reviews and edits a draft before personally sending an individual applicant email; applicants do not converse with the AI product. These are individual administrative replies, not material published to inform the public about matters of public interest.

### AI-003

The current campaign uses visibly illustrated fictional classroom scenes. They are not depictions of an existing person, campus, object or event presented as an authentic photograph or recording. The supplier's product generates images from prompts; this use is not limited to minor correction of supplied images. The published-copy metadata conflict in REC-003 remains unresolved.

### AI-004

Learners interact directly with the licensed spoken-practice product and receive pronunciation feedback. It evaluates spoken pronunciation; it is not used to identify or infer emotions or intentions or to assign people to categories from biometric data. The missing first-interaction notice remains as reported.

### AI-005

The product flags assessment-session activity for a human reviewer and never makes the final assessment decision. Current-release documentation has not yet established whether the flagging uses biometric data to infer emotions, intentions or personal categories. Assessment Operations must supply those facts together with the current development and release records; a human-review step does not settle them.

### AI-006

Staff generate internal meeting summaries within the restricted staff workspace. The summaries are not published or sent to applicants, learners or the general public. They do not involve generated or manipulated images, audio or video.

### AI-007

Visitors converse directly with the licensed website guide. Its visible character is an obviously stylized fictional graphic, not a likeness or recording of an existing person. The graphic alone does not establish what visitors understand about the conversation. The owner/banner and current-capture conflict in REC-008 remains unresolved.

### AI-008

Communications uses the supplier's product to create translated speech and adjusted lip movements for recorded programme presenters. The presenters are real people; the altered recording makes them appear to speak words in a language they did not actually record, and a viewer could mistake it for an authentic recording without disclosure. The videos communicate programme information, not an evidently artistic, satirical or fictional performance. The visible-label report, untested provenance and unapproved exception request remain exactly as recorded.
